News 1: Brann Advances in Conference League Qualifying

Thursday evening saw a fantastic atmosphere at Brann Stadion in Bergen. Brann played the return leg against Romanian side Universitatea Cluj in the second qualifying round of the UEFA Conference League. The first leg in Romania ended 2-2, so everything was still up for grabs before the match in Bergen.

The match kicked off at 7 PM, and Brann took the lead after just eight minutes. Belgian defender Denzel De Roeve scored a beautiful goal to put the home side ahead. The approximately 15,000 spectators in the stadium cheered loudly. But the visitors did not give up. In the 26th minute, the Romanian player Lukić equalized for Cluj, and it was 1-1 at half-time.

In the second half, Brann dominated the match. Just before half-time, in stoppage time, Niklas Castro scored his first goal of the evening, putting Brann back in the lead at 2-1. Brann pushed for more, and in the 75th minute, Castro scored his second goal of the match. The game ended 3-1 to Brann, and 5-3 on aggregate.

This means Brann advances to the third qualifying round of the Conference League. Their next opponent is Apollon Limassol from Cyprus. The first leg will be played at Brann Stadion on August 6, with the return leg in Cyprus on August 13. This is the fourth year in a row that Brann has played European qualifiers at Stadion, and the people of Bergen have grown accustomed to European opposition.

Brann coach Eirik Horneland was pleased after the match. He particularly praised the crowd for the fantastic atmosphere. "This was a team victory. The players worked hard for 90 minutes, and the crowd carried us forward. Now we look forward to facing Apollon," Horneland told the press after the match.

For Brann fans, this is a big day. The club has struggled somewhat in the Eliteserien this season and sits in eighth place, but in Europe the dream lives on. Now all Bergen residents hope the team can go even further in the tournament.

The Conference League is the third-tier European club competition, after the Champions League and Europa League. The tournament was launched in 2021 to give clubs from smaller countries a better chance to play in European competition. For Norwegian teams, the Conference League is an important platform, providing both sporting experience and financial income.

Brann has bounced back after a difficult start to the season. The team was in the lower half of the table in May but has won several important matches recently. In the Eliteserien, they face Rosenborg at Stadion this Sunday, August 2, so it will be a busy week for the players. But first, they can enjoy the victory over the Romanians.

Reaching the group stage of the Conference League would be a major achievement for Brann. It would mean at least six more matches against teams from other European countries. The last Norwegian team to reach the group stage of a European competition was Bodø/Glimt, who have done so several times in recent years. Now Brann dreams of following in their footsteps.

News 2: Norway Challenges Trump's New Tariffs

Norway faces a new trade challenge with the United States. President Donald Trump has imposed a new tariff of 12.5 percent on many goods that the US imports from Norway. This is bad news for Norwegian export industries, and the government is responding quickly.

Foreign Minister Espen Barth Eide has called the tariff "unfair" and says Norway will challenge it. According to newsinenglish.no, Norwegian authorities are now gathering business leaders to plan how to address the new situation. The meeting will take place in Oslo in the coming days.

Norway is not a member of the EU but is part of the EEA agreement. Norway therefore has a special relationship with both the EU and the US when it comes to trade. The new US tariff could affect many Norwegian businesses, particularly in fisheries, the metal industry, and technology. Norwegian salmon is one of the largest export products to the US, and a 12.5 percent tariff could make Norwegian salmon more expensive for American consumers.

The government is concerned that this could lead to fewer jobs in Norwegian export industries. "We must defend Norwegian interests and Norwegian jobs," Barth Eide said in a statement. He also emphasized that Norway wants to continue its good cooperation with the US, but that the tariff is a problem that must be resolved.

The situation is particularly difficult because Norway has previously had a good trade relationship with the US. American consumers buy Norwegian salmon worth billions of kroner every year. Norwegian technology, oil equipment, and metal products are also popular in the American market. A 12.5 percent tariff could mean that American customers choose cheaper alternatives from other countries.

Norwegian fisheries are especially concerned. The salmon industry employs thousands of people along the coast, especially in Northern Norway and Western Norway. If sales to the US decline, it could have major consequences for local communities that depend on fisheries. Several mayors in coastal municipalities have already expressed concern.

Experts say Norway has several options. The country can negotiate directly with the US, or cooperate with the EU on a joint response. Some also believe Norway should consider countermeasures against American goods. Whatever happens, this is an important issue for the Norwegian economy in the time ahead. Trade conflicts between countries have become more common in recent years, and small countries like Norway must be smart to protect their interests.

This case shows how dependent Norway is on international trade. As a small country with an open economy, Norway is vulnerable to changes in global trade. Both politicians and business leaders are therefore following the situation closely.
